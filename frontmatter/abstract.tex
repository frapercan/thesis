\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

Fewer than 0.02\,\% of the proteins deposited in UniProt carry experimentally
validated functional annotations. Computational methods try to close this gap by
transferring Gene Ontology (GO) terms from characterised proteins to the rest, but how
well they do it depends on how they are measured: most published benchmarks let the
answer leak into the question, and most tools are research artefacts that cannot be
re-run when the databases move on.

This doctoral thesis presents \textbf{PROTEA} (\textit{PROtein funcTional
Embedding-based Annotation}), designed and maintained as author and sole maintainer by
Francisco Miguel P\'{e}rez Canales, and the research line it serves. PROTEA is an
open-source platform built as an \emph{instrument}. For every cut, a date, it keeps a
photo of the corpus of that date: the Swiss-Prot entries of the cut, the proteins with
experimental or curated annotation, and the annotated UniRef50 neighbours of Swiss-Prot
proteins, from 1.13 million proteins in 2016 to 1.85 million in 2025, with every GOA
row classed by its evidence channel. It runs every method under one contract, with the
provenance of every result, and it evaluates them on windows that follow the rules of
the LAFA benchmark exactly, by knowledge category, GO branch, sequence identity and
sequence length. A contracts-first plugin architecture spans seven repositories, so
that new embedding backends, annotation sources and methods can be added without
modifying the core.

The research line asks how protein function is best predicted, and answers by stages.
Eight protein language models give the representations of the protein; used for
nearest-neighbour transfer on the validation window GOA~211~$\to$~227, they differ by
less than a hundredth (weighted micro $F$ from 0.3149 to 0.3241, mean of the nine
cells). Candidate terms come from five mechanisms: what the protein already says about
itself, transfer from its neighbours, association between terms, the dynamics of
knowledge, and what is frequent. The strongest source measured so far is the protein's
own: its non-experimental annotations, ordered by a model fitted on the earlier window
194~$\to$~211, reach 0.3543. The signals combine engineered features with the project's
own encodings of the ontology, of the annotations and of the neighbourhood. At the
centre of the line stands one comparison between two learners that read the same
evidence: decision trees that score each candidate term, and a world model, a
joint-embedding predictive architecture that predicts the future knowledge state of a
protein and reads every term from it. The line closes with an evaluation by strata, a
routing of the best combination per stratum learned in validation, and a single clean
read of the frozen method on the held-out test window 227~$\to$~230.

The experimental work was a component of a team ranking of \#19 in CAFA~6. On the LAFA
continuously updated public benchmark, measured once on the sealed September~2025 to
March~2026 target frame and scored with the benchmark's own harness, the offline
research champion of an earlier campaign reached \textbf{first place} overall
(IA-weighted micro-$F$ of 0.391, against 0.381 for the previous leader).

The doctoral contribution is threefold: (1) PROTEA as an instrument for protein
function prediction over time, with a corpus photo per cut, an evaluator faithful to
the benchmark's rules, stratified and traceable results, and a seven-repository plugin
ecosystem; (2) a research line that places every component explored (representations,
sources of evidence, signals, learners and routing) in one frame, and compares a world
model of protein knowledge against decision trees on equal terms; (3) the measurements
themselves, reproducible from the platform, with an independently verified first place
on the LAFA continuous benchmark.

\bigskip
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} protein function prediction, gene ontology, temporal
evaluation, protein language models, nearest-neighbour transfer, learning to rank,
decision trees, joint-embedding predictive architecture, world models, reproducibility,
research infrastructure, bioinformatics.
