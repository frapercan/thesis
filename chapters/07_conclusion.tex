\section{Summary of Contributions}
\label{sec:conclusion-summary}

This thesis set out to demonstrate that a contracts-first, plugin-driven
platform for protein function annotation can simultaneously deliver a
reproducible and leakage-free evaluation methodology, quantifiable gains
from feature-engineered re-ranking over PLM-embedding baselines, and
independently verifiable deployment as a public benchmark submission.
PROTEA (PROtein functional Embedding-based Annotation) is the system that
validates this claim.

The annotation gap that motivated this work remains wide: as of early 2026,
fewer than 0.02\,\% of the approximately 250 million sequences in UniProt
carry experimentally validated \gls{go} annotations~\cite{uniprot2023}.
Computational transfer from characterised proteins to unknown ones narrows
this gap, but only when carried out on a platform that is reproducible,
auditable, and extensible as PLMs and annotation databases evolve. Building
such a platform was the principal research engineering challenge of the
thesis; the empirical evaluation then tested whether the platform enables
science that a notebook-based workflow cannot.

The contributions are organised along three pillars.

\paragraph{Pillar 1: Platform and Infrastructure.}

PROTEA provides a production-grade annotation service backed by a
queue-based worker architecture and a REST API exposing four domains
(Embeddings, Scoring, Annotations, Jobs). Plugin extensibility is
implemented via Python \texttt{entry\_points} across seven code repositories
(\texttt{protea-contracts}, \texttt{-method}, \texttt{-sources},
\texttt{-runners}, \texttt{-backends}, \texttt{-reranker-lab}, and the core
platform), so new annotation sources, runners, and \gls{plm} backends can
be integrated without modifying the core.

A versioned relational data model links every prediction permanently to
the exact ontology release, annotation set, embedding configuration,
dataset, re-ranker model, and code commit that produced it. The temporal
holdout training pipeline is fully parameterised: re-running the same
payload against the same database state reproduces identical models and
results. This addresses the reproducibility gap identified in
\cref{chap:related}.

Deployment flexibility is achieved through four graduated modes, all
sharing the same canonical pipeline without code branching: a development
\texttt{docker compose} bundle (T-OPS.2), a Helm chart for Kubernetes
(T-OPS.3), a Docker Swarm stack file (T-OPS.4), and SLURM~\texttt{sbatch}
templates for \gls{hpc} environments (T-OPS.5). An end-to-end CI deploy test
(T-OPS.11) gates every release against image-level worker regressions.

Observability is provided by an OpenTelemetry SDK boot layer and five
Grafana dashboards covering API latency, queue depths, worker throughput,
database connections, and embedding-store load. Deeper SQLAlchemy and pika
instrumentation with distributed \texttt{traceparent} propagation is in
review at time of writing.

\paragraph{Pillar 2: Research Methods.}

The feature-engineering layer is organised as a registry of 56 features
per candidate annotation, augmenting embedding cosine similarity with
Needleman-Wunsch and Smith-Waterman alignment statistics,
NCBI-taxonomy-based distance metrics, \gls{go} \gls{dag} ancestry
embeddings, and per-\gls{plm} PCA projections. The lineage-feature wire
(T-RES.1b, PR~\#304) connects GO directed-acyclic-graph relation
features to the re-ranker training pipeline and regenerates the golden
parquet artefact used to gate regressions.

The protea-method inference layer received a gated-attention
\gls{mil} head (MIL.2a, PR~\#13 in
\texttt{protea-method}) that provides an opt-in pooling alternative to
mean aggregation for bag-of-patch embeddings, extending the hypothesis
space for future architectural comparisons.

A selective learning-to-rank re-ranker is trained per category
(No-Knowledge, Limited-Knowledge, Prior-Knowledge) and per aspect
(\gls{bpo}, \gls{mfo}, \gls{cco}) using LightGBM over the feature registry, with a
selective-application policy that falls back to the embedding-only
baseline whenever re-ranking would degrade performance for the current
cell. Training follows a temporal holdout protocol spanning twelve
consecutive GOA splits with Information Accretion weighting.

\paragraph{Pillar 3: Empirical Evaluation.}

The benchmark of eight \gls{plm} backends (ESM-2 in three sizes, ESM-C,
ProstT5, ProtT5, Ankh, and the ESM-3 component encoder) on a common
evaluation surface yielded an average lab $F_{\max}$ of \textbf{0.2881}
(3-seed replication, 56-feature schema) and a cafaeval score of
\textbf{0.5427} on the GOA~220~$\to$~229 temporal split. The 56-feature
re-ranker significantly outperforms the raw KNN baseline in all nine
category-aspect cells (paired bootstrap, all $p < 0.0001$). A comparison with
eggNOG-mapper v2.1.13 demonstrates that the PROTEA embedding-only baseline
surpasses DIAMOND-based orthology transfer in eight of nine cells, and the
leakage-free full-feature re-ranker substantially exceeds both in the NK and LK
categories. The binary-label per-category LightGBM champion, replicated across
three seeds on \texttt{bench-v1-K5-v226-lineage-prostt5}, achieves
NK+LK cafaeval $F_{\max}$ of \textbf{0.7291 $\pm$ 0.0028}
(LB.3 paired bootstrap, 6/6 NK+LK cells strictly positive at 95\%
confidence), and is the primary publishable result for the NK and LK
categories. The multi-PLM $\times$ $K \in \{3,5,10\}$ generalisation grid
(FARM-EXP.13, 24 datasets) provides the empirical breadth context for the
thesis; 15 of 24 cells are complete and confirm the champion result generalises
across PLMs. The completed grid cells are being packaged as FAIR-compliant
datasets via the F-DATA-PACK loop.

The CAFA~6 challenge provided an independent blind validation: the research
team in which the author of this thesis served as technical lead achieved a
second-place team ranking, confirming that the methodology developed here is
competitive with the best publicly evaluated alternatives under the strictest
possible evaluation conditions.

\section{Answers to the Research Questions}
\label{sec:conclusion-rq}

\textbf{RQ1}: \emph{Can embedding-based KNN annotation transfer match or
exceed alignment-based methods for GO term prediction, particularly at low
sequence identity, and how does the answer change as new PLMs replace older
ones?}

The embedding-only baseline with $k{=}5$ neighbours reaches $F_{\max}$ values
between 0.187 (PK-BPO) and 0.668 (NK-CCO) without any sequence alignment.
Adding alignment features via the re-ranker improves performance by up to
$+0.049$~$F_{\max}$ (LK-MFO), indicating that embeddings and alignments capture
partially orthogonal signals. A direct comparison with eggNOG-mapper~v2.1.13
confirms that PROTEA's embedding-based approach outperforms \gls{diamond}-based
orthology transfer in all nine category-aspect cells, with absolute
improvements of up to $+0.389$~$F_{\max}$ (NK-CCO, leakage-free full-feature
re-ranker vs eggNOG-mapper). The hybrid-picker ceiling
of 0.4400 $F_{\max}$ further demonstrates that combining backends yields
headroom beyond any single \gls{plm}.

\textbf{RQ2}: \emph{Do pairwise alignment statistics, taxonomic distance,
ontology-graph ancestry features, and per-PLM PCA projections provide
complementary signal that improves the ranking of candidate annotations once
the embedding baseline is already strong?}

Yes, though the dominant signal comes from ontology-derived features rather
than classical alignment. The leave-one-family-out ablation (Exp.~8,
\cref{tab:exp8-ablation}) shows that the \texttt{anc2vec\_query} family
contributes a mean $\Delta F_{\max}$ of $+0.1449$ across three representative
cells ($+0.2565$ on NK-BPO alone), while every other family contributes at
most $\pm 0.01$ on average. Alignment and taxonomy features were confirmed to
carry signal in the progression from the per-category re-ranker (where they were
absent and the model failed to surpass the heuristic) to the full-feature
re-ranker (where their inclusion stabilised training and enabled large NK and LK
gains), but the
dominant marginal contribution comes from ontology structure, not sequence
similarity. Raw embedding distance does not appear in the top-10 features for
any category, confirming that the re-ranker treats PLM similarity as a
candidate-generation filter rather than a final ranking signal.

\textbf{RQ3}: \emph{How can a reproducible, scalable annotation pipeline be
designed so that researchers can re-run analyses as ontologies and databases
are updated, and so that the pipeline can be deployed in development, cloud,
on-premise HPC, and airgapped environments without code branching?}

\begin{sloppypar}
PROTEA's versioned data model links every prediction to an immutable
\texttt{EmbeddingConfig} \gls{uuid}, a versioned \texttt{AnnotationSet}, and a dated
\texttt{OntologySnapshot}. The temporal holdout training pipeline is fully
parameterised: re-running the same payload against the same database state
reproduces identical models and results. When new GOA releases become available,
the pipeline extends by appending the new version and shifting the test window.
Deployment portability across four target environments (docker compose, Helm,
Swarm, SLURM) without code branching, combined with end-to-end CI gating
(T-OPS.11), demonstrates that reproducibility and operational scalability are
achievable simultaneously.
\end{sloppypar}

\textbf{RQ4}: \emph{How can the journey of a research platform, with its dead
ends and pivots, be captured operationally so that the canonical pipeline is
justified by an auditable trace rather than by post-hoc rationalisation?}

The \texttt{Job.findings} narrative model records the why of every run
alongside the what of every computed result. Every job writes its inputs
(ontology version, annotation release, embedding configuration, search
parameters) to a structured database record before execution begins; findings
written during execution are attached as structured observations. Complementing
this, an index of 38 Architecture Decision Records (D1 through D38) in
\texttt{protea-core/docs/source/adr/} provides a curated history of
non-obvious design choices, including decisions later reversed (D09, D13,
D17) and decisions to defer a research direction (D38, neural-head
deferral). The progression from the per-aspect through the 56-feature re-ranker
(Experiments~4 to~8) and onward to the binary-label champion replication
(Experiment~9) is fully traceable through experiment run records, not
reconstructed from memory: the evaluation chapter is distilled from the database,
not from notes.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:conclusion-limitations}

\begin{description}
  \item[Single hardware configuration.] All embedding and re-ranker experiments
    run on a single workstation with a 16\,GB GPU. Large-scale multi-GPU
    experiments and distributed training are out of scope. The NumPy-based exact
    KNN search is tractable for the 527,000-sequence Swiss-Prot reference set
    but would require replacement with FAISS or a distributed approximate
    backend at TrEMBL scale.

  \item[Single primary embedding model.] Main experiments use ESMC~300M; the
    8-backend benchmark shows the ceiling moves with model size and family, but
    a full ensemble at inference time is not yet deployed.

  \item[No structural information.] Protein tertiary structure provides
    functional signal beyond sequence alone. Integrating structure-based
    embeddings (e.g.\ from ESM-IF or Foldseek) is a natural extension not yet
    pursued.

  \item[Limited external tool comparison.] The evaluation includes a comparison
    with eggNOG-mapper but does not yet cover BLAST or InterProScan-based
    annotations under the same leakage-free temporal protocol. The InterPro
    integration (IP.2/IP.3/IP.4a) provides the data foundation for this
    comparison in future work.

  \item[Benchmark coverage.] The temporal holdout covers proteins that received
    experimental annotations in a fixed window (GOA~220~$\to$~229). This may
    over-represent proteins that are easy to annotate and under-represent orphan
    proteins in the evaluation set.

  \item[Pending observability work.] Deep SQLAlchemy and pika instrumentation
    with distributed \texttt{traceparent} propagation (T5.1b, PR~\#330) is open
    at time of writing and is not reflected in the production metrics dashboards.

  \item[PK cell performance.] The selective re-ranker degrades on PK-BPO
    relative to the embedding-only baseline after the leakage fix, reflecting
    the stricter positive-rate criterion in PK; the fallback policy partially
    mitigates this but a cell-specific tuning pass is warranted.
\end{description}

\section{Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion-future-work}

The results of this thesis open four concrete research directions for
post-defence development, each foreshadowed by the empirical findings or by
architectural decisions described in earlier chapters.

\paragraph{Neural Architecture Exploration: Explored but Not Shipped.}
\label{para:future-neural-boundary}

The leave-one-family-out ablation (Exp.~8) established that the
\texttt{anc2vec\_query} family is the single most valuable feature family by a
large margin (mean $\Delta F_{\max} = +0.1449$,
Section~\ref{subsec:exp8-reranker-56f}), with GO-DAG-derived embeddings
supplying ontology structural information that protein sequence embeddings do
not encode. This observation motivated an exploratory deep-learning run: a
frozen ESMC-300M encoder with a binary classification head was trained end-to-end
on 84\,000 proteins over ten epochs (binary NK+LK task, bf16 precision, chunk
size 128). The run completed within the thesis timeline. However, its internal
cafaeval $F_{\max}$ did not surpass the LightGBM champion (0.7291 $\pm$ 0.0028,
Exp.~9) within the available GPU budget: the neural head would need to capture
qualitatively new signal beyond the nine aspect-stable generalist features that
already dominate the champion, rather than re-learning the same vote-aggregation
and ontology-proximity signals through a different architecture (ADR-D38,
2026-05-25).

The decision recorded in ADR-D38 is therefore to defer the neural head
indefinitely. The prerequisite for resumption is a demonstrated gap of at
least 0.005 $F_{\max}$ units between the LightGBM ceiling on the
v226-to-v230 generalisation window and a retrained neural head on the same
benchmark, a threshold structurally parallel to the GeOKG NO-GO precondition
(2026-05-17). The neural-head scaffold is preserved locally as an exploratory
artefact; it is not wired to any PROTEA registry row or any thesis table.
If a future architecture (e.g.\ a Relational Graph Convolutional Network
directly over the GO DAG, or a protein-GO joint message-passing model) clears
this bar under the same leakage-free temporal protocol, it would slot into the
PROTEA plugin architecture via the \texttt{Scorer} ABC without requiring
changes to the evaluation harness.

\paragraph{Curated Dataset Packaging: the F-DATA-PACK Loop.}
\label{para:future-fdata-pack}

The most concrete and bounded near-term deliverable is the F-DATA-PACK loop,
which packages the 24-dataset multi-PLM grid produced by FARM-EXP.13 as
FAIR-compliant, externally reproducible research datasets. The loop comprises
four sequential tasks:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Manifest validator} (F-DATA-PACK.1, lab PR~\#48, merged):
    a schema-and-content-hash checker wired into the PROTEA export pipeline
    that ensures every exported dataset carries a verified manifest before any
    downstream step can consume it.
  \item \textbf{Per-dataset READMEs and dataset cards}: one README per
    (PLM, $K$) cell following the HuggingFace \texttt{DatasetCard} and Zenodo
    metadata schemas, documenting the data provenance, split logic, feature
    schema, and citation information needed for independent reproduction.
  \item \textbf{FAIR documentation page}: a Sphinx page under
    \texttt{docs/source/datasets/} aggregating provenance metadata, coverage
    statistics, and usage instructions for all 24 datasets in a single
    citable location.
  \item \textbf{Zenodo and/or HuggingFace Hub upload}: archival upload of the
    full grid, establishing persistent DOIs that allow any reader of
    this thesis or of a derived publication to reproduce the evaluation
    without access to the local PROTEA instance.
\end{enumerate}

Completing F-DATA-PACK before thesis submission provides a tangible,
externally verifiable artefact that strengthens the reproducibility claim of
Chapter~\ref{chap:evaluation} independently of any further training runs.
It also directly benefits any future researcher who wants to evaluate
an alternative re-ranker recipe, a new PLM, or a neural architecture on
the same grid: the datasets are ready to use without re-running the
computationally expensive PROTEA export pipeline.

\paragraph{GeOKG Generalisation.}
\label{para:future-geokg}

The related work chapter (\cref{chap:related}) discusses GeOKG~\cite{geokg2025}
as a multi-curvature space (combining hyperbolic and Euclidean geometry) for
embedding the GO DAG, motivated by the observation that the GO DAG has both
tree-like and grid-like substructure that no single-geometry embedding captures
well. At time of writing, GeOKG-style embeddings have not yet been integrated
into PROTEA's feature registry; anc2vec remains the production GO-embedding
family.

The D19 Architecture Decision Record identifies GeOKG integration as a one-week
conditional research target: if its geometry-aware embeddings outperform
anc2vec in a controlled ablation on the NK-BPO cell (where
\texttt{anc2vec\_query} contributes the most), they should replace anc2vec in
the canonical feature set. A full integration would: (i)~train GeOKG on the
same GO ontology snapshot used for the evaluation (\texttt{releases/2026-01-23});
(ii)~register the resulting embeddings in the \texttt{FeatureRegistry} as a
new family; (iii)~run the leave-one-family-out ablation (following Exp.~8) with
GeOKG replacing anc2vec to produce a clean head-to-head delta under the
leakage-free
protocol. If GeOKG generalises beyond the specific GO snapshot it was trained
on, it also opens the path to cross-ontology transfer where the same embedding
model covers GO, HPO, and ChEBI simultaneously.

\paragraph{Ensemble Multi-K Inference.}
\label{para:future-multik}

Experiment~1 (\cref{subsec:exp1-k}) established that $k{=}5$ minimises
$F_{\max}$ degradation from noise in the KNN neighbourhood, with performance
declining monotonically as $k$ grows. However, the hybrid-picker analysis
demonstrates a ceiling of 0.4400 $F_{\max}$ when the optimal backend is
selected per category-aspect cell, revealing that different K values may be
optimal for different query proteins even within a single cell. A multi-K
ensemble would aggregate predictions from $k \in \{5, 10, 20\}$ independent
KNN sweeps, using a meta-classifier (or a lightweight bandit algorithm) to
weight each K's predictions based on query-level signals such as the density
of the local embedding neighbourhood, the variance of cosine distances among
the top-$k$ candidates, and the annotation density of the retrieved
neighbours. This extends the current single-K sweep into a richer inference
strategy without architectural changes to the pipeline: each K sweep is a
separate \texttt{Job}, and their outputs are combined by an additional
aggregation operation registered in the \texttt{OperationRegistry}.

The $3^3$ hyperparameter sensitivity study on NK-BPO (\cref{subsec:exp8-reranker-56f})
showed a 0.0283 spread across configurations, bounding the expected gain from
search-strategy optimisation. A multi-K ensemble represents a complementary
axis of improvement and is distinct from backend ensembling: it exploits
geometric properties of the embedding space rather than differences between PLM
representations.

\paragraph{Granular Plugin Split (F9 Horizon).}
\label{para:future-f9-plugin}

The current seven-repository split (core, contracts, method, sources,
backends, runners, reranker-lab) was designed around operational concerns
identified in D14 and D31. As the platform matures and external contributors
become a realistic audience, a finer-grained decomposition of the three plugin
repositories (\texttt{protea-sources}, \texttt{protea-backends},
\texttt{protea-runners}) is warranted. The F9 horizon would split each plugin
repository into one package per plugin instance (e.g.\
\texttt{protea-backend-esmc}, \texttt{protea-backend-esm2},
\texttt{protea-source-quickgo}, \texttt{protea-source-uniprot}), allowing
consumers to depend on only the specific integrations they require without
pulling in GPU libraries for backends they never use.

This decomposition has implications for versioning: each plugin package would
carry its own SemVer tag, and the contracts package would define the minimum
version of each ABC that a backend must satisfy. The \texttt{protea-contracts}
package already functions as the SemVer-versioned extension surface; extending
the same discipline to per-plugin packages would make the dependency graph
explicit and auditable. The reranker-lab would remain a single offline package
to avoid fragmenting the training pipeline. This restructuring is foreshadowed
by the discussion of plugin granularity in \cref{chap:design}
(Section~\ref{sec:plugin-contracts}) and does not require changes to the core
platform or the contracts package, only to the distribution and tagging
strategy of the existing plugin code.

\section{Closing Remarks}
\label{sec:conclusion-closing}

The central claim of this thesis was methodological: that a contracts-first,
plugin-driven platform can serve simultaneously as a reproducible research
instrument, a competitive annotation method, and an independently deployable
artefact. The evaluation results, the LAFA container submissions, and the CAFA~6
team result together constitute the evidence for this claim.

The annotation gap in protein biology is not a problem that will be solved by
any single method or any single snapshot of available PLMs. The value of
PROTEA as a platform, as opposed to a published result, lies precisely in its
capacity to be re-evaluated as the field evolves. Every new PLM that outperforms
ESMC~300M can be plugged in as a new \texttt{EmbeddingBackend}; every new GOA
release extends the temporal holdout window; every new feature family can be
registered without touching existing models or breaking the schema fingerprint
mechanism. The architecture was designed to make scientific claims about protein
function prediction revisitable and independently verifiable, not just now but
as the underlying technology changes.

The four future-work directions described above are not disconnected from the
thesis results. They follow directly from the empirical findings: the
F-DATA-PACK loop closes the reproducibility loop opened by the multi-PLM
grid in Experiment~9; the GeOKG direction addresses the saturation of the
\texttt{anc2vec\_query} family identified in the feature ablation; the
multi-K ensemble exploits the geometric structure of the embedding space
revealed by the $k$-sweep; and the granular plugin split addresses the
growing consumer audience of the platform. The neural-head boundary
experiment, explored within the thesis timeline and recorded in ADR-D38,
establishes a concrete quantitative precondition that any deep-learning
alternative must meet before it displaces the binary-label LightGBM champion
as the canonical method. Each direction can be pursued independently,
evaluated under the same leakage-free temporal protocol, and contributed to
the platform without breaking existing functionality. This is the property
that makes the engineering contribution of the thesis durable beyond the
specific numbers reported in \cref{chap:evaluation}.
