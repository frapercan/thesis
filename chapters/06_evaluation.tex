\chapter{Evaluation}
\label{chap:evaluation}

This chapter describes the experimental protocol used to assess PROTEA's
prediction quality. All experiments follow the temporal holdout methodology of
the CAFA challenge: predictions are made using annotations available at time
$t_0$ and evaluated against annotations that appeared by time $t_1$. The holdout
is expressed as a window between two dated GOA releases (the public EBI archive
records the release dates), and it is the same protocol LAFA uses for its rolling
submission windows: predict from the annotations known at the earlier date and
score against the later date. CAFA~6 was a one-shot evaluation over a fixed
target set; the continuously re-runnable temporal-evaluation environment
described here is post-CAFA follow-up work. Its contribution is not a single
score but an environment that measures honestly, without temporal leakage, and
that lets the system keep closing the gap to the state of the art as new GOA
releases land. In keeping with the discipline of this thesis, every reported
number has a live source on the PROTEA user interface.

\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:eval-setup}

\subsection{Hardware and Software}

All experiments run on a single workstation equipped with an NVIDIA \gls{gpu}
(16\,GB VRAM), 64\,GB system RAM, and a PostgreSQL~16 instance with the
\texttt{pgvector} extension. Embeddings are computed with ESMC~300M
(\texttt{esmc\_300m}, 960-dimensional output) using mean pooling over residue
representations.

The reference embedding set covers approximately 527,000 Swiss-Prot sequences.
\Gls{knn} search is performed in Python using NumPy (exact cosine distance) for
all baseline experiments.

\subsection{The Experimental Ladder}
\label{subsec:ladder}

The experiments in this chapter are not a survey. They are a sequence, and
the sequence has a rule: each step varies one thing and holds everything
else at a value an earlier step chose. A step is called a rung, and the
constants a rung holds are the answers of the rungs below it.

That rule is what makes the sequence load-bearing rather than decorative.
If a rung's answer later changes, every rung that held it constant is
invalidated, and the dependency is written down so that the invalidation is
visible rather than remembered. A ladder whose steps do not declare what
they stand on is a list of experiments in chronological order.

The rungs are declared before they run, in a form that is checked
automatically: what each one varies, named down to the field of the job
payload that carries it, and what each one holds, named with the rung that
decided it. A dispatched job whose varied axes contradict its declared rung
is a mistake the declaration can catch.

The cost of not having it is not hypothetical. A grid of forty-eight arms
ran two of its rung's three declared axes; the third, the weighting that
turns neighbour evidence into a score, was never varied. It was not set to
a default that had been chosen, but to the value the code falls back on
when no scoring configuration is supplied, which is arithmetically the
weakest of the seven the system offers. Nothing in the grid, the artifacts
or the published tables recorded that a declared axis had gone unswept,
and the omission was found only when someone asked what the numbers were
measuring.

\paragraph{How every rung reports.}
No result in this chapter is reported over the whole population. Every
figure is given per stratum, and the stratum that matters most is the
sequence identity of the query to its nearest annotated donor.

The reason is measured rather than assumed. Across the eight
representations at the winning neighbourhood size, in the no-knowledge
category, the spread between the best and worst representation is
$0.0848$ where no donor exceeds thirty per cent identity, and $0.0081$
where one exceeds ninety. A single number over the whole population is
dominated by the second case, which is the case that needs no help: there,
a model of eight million parameters scores within four thousandths of one
with six hundred and fifty million. Where no close donor exists, the same
small model is last by eight and a half hundredths. A conclusion drawn from
the pooled figure is therefore true of the proteins the method already
handles and false of the proteins it exists for.

Two consequences follow, and both are rules rather than observations.

First, stratifying by sequence length does not discharge this requirement.
Measured on the same arms, the representation spread by length band is
$0.0323$ and $0.0388$ in the two bands that carry data, which is a tie,
while the largest spread inside each of those bands is the same figure as
the twilight maximum. Length does not separate the effect; it scatters it.
A table stratified by length carries the appearance of the discipline
without its content, which makes it worse than an unstratified table,
because it invites the reader to believe the check has been done.

Second, the identity band excludes the query's own sequence, and excludes
it by sequence rather than by accession, because isoforms, re-annotations
and cross-references place one sequence in the reference set under several
names. Including the self match moves the median identity of the evaluation
cohort from forty-one per cent to one hundred, and places the great
majority of the cohort in the near-identical band, which empties every
other band and makes the axis unreadable.

\paragraph{What the swept axes are worth.}
The ladder's first rung sweeps three things: the representation, the
number of neighbours retrieved, and the weighting that turns neighbour
evidence into a score. All three have now been measured on one frame, and
the result is worth stating as a result rather than as preparation for a
fourth.

Taking the best configuration each axis can reach, against the best the
others can reach without it, the three are worth the following:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lc}
\toprule
Axis & Best-to-best difference \\
\midrule
Score weighting     & $0.0000$ to $0.0088$ \\
Neighbourhood size  & a few thousandths \\
Representation      & $0.0221$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

None of them moves the operating point by more than a hundredth. Against
that, which identity band a query falls into is worth a factor of four,
and it is not a setting: nothing the method chooses moves a protein from
one band to another.

Two of these figures are easy to misread and both were misread here first.

The score weighting appears far larger than this when averaged over
neighbourhood sizes, where the spread between weightings reaches $0.0485$.
That average is dominated by configurations nobody would deploy. The
spread between weightings is $0.0054$ at one neighbour and $0.1710$ at
thirty, because at one neighbour there is a single donor per term and
nothing for a weighting to discriminate between. What the weighting buys
is therefore not accuracy but tolerance: under the weakest weighting,
going from one neighbour to thirty costs sixty-eight per cent, and under
the best it costs fourteen. It raises no ceiling.

The representation is the reverse case. Aggregated it is the smallest
interesting number in this chapter, and split by identity band it is the
largest lever the campaign has found: $0.0848$ where no donor exceeds
thirty per cent identity, against $0.0081$ where one exceeds ninety. In
the easy band a model of eight million parameters scores within four
thousandths of one with six hundred and fifty million; in the hard band
the same model is last by eight and a half hundredths.

An independent measurement agrees. Reachability of the gained terms from
a short candidate list, computed from raw retrieval rows without any
scoring or thresholding, gives a spread across the same eight
representations of $0.0842$ in the same band. The two quantities are not
the same: one is a set membership before any threshold, the other a
threshold-optimised F. Their proximity was examined and no mechanism was
found for it, and it is not treated as evidence here. They are two
measurements that agree, reported as such.

Only the spread transfers, and this is worth stating because the
temptation runs the other way. The same reachability measured against a
different donor bank moves from $0.665$ to $0.602$ while the ordering and
the band structure hold. The absolute level is therefore a property of who
is in the reference set rather than of protein function or of the method,
and no absolute reachability figure is quoted in this work for that
reason.

\paragraph{What a code has to keep.}
One result from this rung is directly actionable in what is served, and
reaching it required correcting the same measurement twice.

Quantising the served sparse code and measuring retrieval over the whole
no-knowledge cell, against a bank of eighty-six thousand experimentally
annotated donors, with bands over the full population:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{llrrl}
\toprule
Clip & Bits & Bytes & Change & Bands separating \\
\midrule
99th pct & 8 & 304 & $+0.0014$ & 0 of 4 \\
99th pct & 4 & 240 & $-0.0001$ & 0 of 4 \\
99th pct & 2 & 208 & $-0.0243$ & 4 of 4 \\
99th pct & 1 & 192 & $+0.0015$ & 0 of 4 \\
maximum  & 2 & 208 & $-0.0528$ & 4 of 4 \\
maximum  & 1 & 192 & $-0.0003$ & 1 of 4 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Two bits cost and one bit does not, which is not monotone and has a
measured cause: what costs is deleting atoms, not coarsening their
magnitudes. At two bits the signed grid is $\{-1, 0, +1\}$, so every atom
below half the scale rounds to zero and the code loses most of its
support; sign-only has no zero level to fall into and deletes nothing. Of
the $127.9$ atoms a code carries, four-bit clipping deletes none, two-bit
deletes $81.8$ per cent, and two-bit under max-scaling deletes $91.3$ per
cent, and those figures order exactly with the three losses.

\paragraph{Why the recommendation is four bits and not one.}
On this table one bit is free and saves most, and that is the wrong
reading. It was reached, and then abandoned, for a reason worth reporting.

The apparent $+0.0015$ at one bit is not the quantisation. Decomposed, the
percentile clip alone carries $+0.0012$ with no quantisation applied at
all, and every configuration containing the clip carries about that much
while every configuration without it sits at zero. The clip's own effect
is not distinguishable from nothing, and it was being attributed to the
width.

Chasing that offset surfaced the real objection. Sign-only under the two
clips should be mathematically identical, because the scale is a positive
per-row scalar that a cosine normalises away, and the two code matrices do
agree to $1.5 \times 10^{-8}$. Their retrieval nonetheless differed. The
only thing a perturbation that small can change is a tie:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrr}
\toprule
Variant & Donors tied at the cut & Neighbours differing of 30 \\
\midrule
Unquantised & 1.0 & 0 \\
4 bits & 1.0 & 4.5 \\
8 bits & 1.1 & 4.0 \\
1 bit & 8.4 & 9.6 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

At one bit every code carries the same magnitudes and differs only in
sign, so the cosine takes few distinct values and the ranking degenerates.
Eight donors sit tied exactly at the cut, and a third of the returned
neighbourhood is decided by floating-point noise rather than by
similarity.

Set reachability does not notice, because it averages over which donors
arrive. Everything downstream does: the re-ranker sees a different
candidate set, a rerun returns different neighbours, and the interface
shows the donor to a reader as the evidence for a prediction. A
neighbourhood that is one third arbitrary is not servable at any size.

So the recommendation is four bits: $240$ bytes against $688$ for float32
indices and values, a saving of sixty-five per cent, free on the metric in
every identity band including twilight, and preserving the tie structure
of the unquantised code exactly.

The one-bit row is worth keeping in the table as an illustration rather
than as an option. A metric that is insensitive to \emph{which} donors
arrive cannot certify a retrieval system whose purpose is to surface them,
and a configuration can be free on the measurement and unusable in the
product for reasons the measurement was never able to see.

One further limit belongs with the result rather than after it. This says
the magnitudes carry little beyond sign and support \emph{for a cosine}.
The same code has a second consumer, a learner that reads every column,
and these two consumers have been measured disagreeing before. Nothing
here licenses a narrow code as a feature vector.

% ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\paragraph{What this leaves.}
The honest reading of the first rung is that the levers it was built to
sweep are small at the point where the method is actually operated, and
that the one large effect it found is a property of the query rather than
a choice available to the method. That is a finding about the shape of the
problem, and it sets what a later rung has to clear: an intervention worth
reporting must move the hard band, because the easy band has been shown to
be insensitive to every choice measured here.

% ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\paragraph{What carries the band where help is needed.}
Naming the axis is not the same as saying what moves along it, and the two
answers a reader would guess are both wrong.

Inside the band where no donor exceeds thirty per cent identity, the
precision at the head of each protein's own ranking is as follows, against
a chance rate of $0.031$:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lc}
\toprule
Score & Precision at rank 1 \\
\midrule
Composite            & 0.239 \\
Neighbour agreement  & 0.220 \\
Embedding similarity & 0.156 \\
Alignment identity   & 0.059 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Alignment identity is the weakest by a wide margin, at less than twice
chance where the others reach five to seven times it. This is not a failure
to discriminate: measured on the same proteins, that score resolves
twenty-six distinct values per protein where neighbour agreement resolves
fourteen, and it is the coarser of the two that ranks better. What it does
is order the candidates almost independently of whether they are correct,
which is what one should expect of a similarity that is not there: below
thirty per cent identity there is no alignment worth reading.

The result that matters is the third row. The embedding's own similarity is
itself a weak ranker in this band. What ranks well is the agreement among
the neighbours the embedding retrieved, a quantity derived from the
embedding and distinct from it.

So in the regime this work exists to serve, the useful signal is neither
sequence identity nor embedding similarity. It is neighbourhood consensus.
That is a narrower claim than the one the method is usually described by,
and it is the one the measurements support.

It also sets what a retrieval strategy has to be judged on. If consensus is
what carries the hard band, then the question about any retriever is
whether the neighbourhood it returns agrees, not whether its first result
is correct. Those are different objectives, and a retriever can satisfy one
while failing the other.

% ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\paragraph{What the identity band is not.}
It is not a setting. Nothing the method chooses moves a protein from one
band to another, because the band is a property of the query and of what
the reference set happens to contain at the temporal cut. Every other axis
in this chapter is something the method selects; this one is the regime it
finds itself in. That difference is why the bands are reported alongside
every result rather than optimised over.

\section{Evaluation Categories (NK/LK/PK)}
\label{subsec:nk-lk-pk}

Following the CAFA5 protocol, test proteins are classified into three
mutually exclusive categories \emph{per (protein, namespace)}, where namespace
is one of Molecular Function (MF), Biological Process (BP), or Cellular
Component (CC).

\begin{description}
  \item[\textbf{NK (No-Knowledge).}]
    The protein had \emph{no} experimental annotations in \emph{any} namespace
    at $t_0$. All new annotations across all namespaces form the NK ground truth.
    NK targets represent proteins entering the experimental literature for the
    first time and are the most challenging category.

  \item[\textbf{LK (Limited-Knowledge).}]
    The protein had experimental annotations in some namespaces at $t_0$ but
    not in namespace $S$. It gained new annotations in $S$ at $t_1$. Those new
    annotations in $S$ are the LK ground truth for the $(P, S)$ pair.

  \item[\textbf{PK (Partial-Knowledge).}]
    The protein already had experimental annotations in namespace $S$ at $t_0$
    and gained additional annotations in $S$ at $t_1$. Only the novel terms are
    ground truth. Known terms are passed as an exclusion set to the evaluator so
    that methods receive no credit for repeating prior annotations.
\end{description}

\section{Metrics}
\label{sec:metrics}

All evaluations use the \texttt{cafaeval} tool with Information Accretion (IA)
weighting derived from the CAFA6 IA file (\texttt{IA\_cafa6.tsv}).

\begin{description}
  \item[$F_{\max}$] The maximum $F_1$ score over all prediction confidence
    thresholds $\tau \in [0, 1]$, computed per GO aspect. Precision and recall
    are defined over the full GO hierarchy using the true path rule.
    $F_{\max}$ is the primary comparison metric throughout this chapter.
\end{description}

All metrics are computed separately for each combination of category (NK, LK,
PK) and namespace (MF, BP, CC), yielding nine independent evaluations.

Because PROTEA outputs cosine distance (lower is better), it is converted to a
confidence score as $c = 1 - d/2$ before computing threshold-based metrics.

\section{Experiments and Results}

per-(PLM, $K$) benchmark numbers are served from the \texttt{/benchmark} surface
and the per-(category, aspect) temporal-split numbers from the

(\texttt{prop=fill, norm=cafa}) over an explicit ($t_0 \to t_1$) GOA window. To
make a number traceable, each result is anchored by its temporal split, its

\subsection{Experiment~10: Universal Reranker}
\label{subsec:exp10-universal}

(\texttt{f\_micro\_w}, computed via \texttt{cafaeval}).

\subsubsection{The \texttt{f\_micro\_w} Metric}
\label{subsubsec:f-micro-w}

All prior experiments in this chapter use the protein-averaged $F_{\max}$
(macro-average: first compute $F_{\max}$ per protein, then average). The live
LAFA leaderboard and the CAFA6 assessment use the IA-weighted
\emph{micro}-average variant, denoted $f\_micro\_w$:

\begin{equation}
  f_{\mathrm{micro\text{-}w}}(\tau) =
    \frac{2 \, \sum_{p,t} w_t \cdot \mathbf{1}[\hat{c}_{p,t} \ge \tau] \cdot \mathbf{1}[t \in T_p^*]}
         {\sum_{p,t} w_t \cdot \mathbf{1}[\hat{c}_{p,t} \ge \tau]
         + \sum_{p,t} w_t \cdot \mathbf{1}[t \in T_p^*]}
\end{equation}

where $w_t$ is the Information Accretion of GO term $t$, $\hat{c}_{p,t}$ is
the predicted confidence score, $T_p^*$ is the set of ground-truth terms for
protein $p$, and the outer maximum over $\tau$ defines $F_{\max}$.

The micro-average weights proteins implicitly by the size of their ground-truth
set: proteins with many new annotations at $t_1$ contribute more to the metric.
IA weighting further concentrates mass on informative terms. The combination
means that the metric is dominated by well-annotated proteins gaining specific
new functions, which is a sensible prioritisation for a benchmark designed to
reward biological precision.

(optimistic configuration: candidate rows include the known correct GO terms).

be combined in a single training run without numerical instability. However,

% ── Experiment 11 ─────────────────────────────────────────────
\subsection{Experiment~11: GOA Self-Prior and LAFA Live-Board Analysis}
\label{subsec:exp11-lafa-goa-prior}

The LAFA benchmark provides a live leaderboard that reveals one systematic gap
not visible in the offline evaluation: the GOA Non-experimental baseline
(\textit{GOA\,Nonexp}) consistently outperforms the bare KNN submission
(\texttt{protea-knn-v1}) in several category-aspect cells. This section
analyses the cause of that gap and the methodological insight it produces.

\subsubsection{Why the GOA Non-experimental Baseline Beats Bare KNN}

The GOA\,Nonexp submission assigns to each target protein its own non-experimental
GO annotations from the $t_0$ release as predictions. Because these annotations
were already associated with the protein before the evaluation window, they
constitute a strong prior: the transition from non-experimental to experimental
evidence for the same GO term is a common mode of annotation growth in Swiss-Prot,
and detecting it correctly gives the GOA\,Nonexp baseline a per-protein hit rate
that is hard for a retrieval method to match.

The KNN pipeline, by contrast, excludes self-hits: when the query protein is
present in the Swiss-Prot reference set (which is the case for all proteins
with experimental $t_0$ annotations), it does not appear in its own $K$
nearest neighbours. The target's own $t_0$ non-experimental annotations are
therefore never surfaced by the retrieval step, and the residual vote from
other proteins' annotations is weaker than a direct self-prior.

The magnitude of the gap depends on the evaluation category:
\begin{itemize}
  \item For \textbf{NK proteins} (no experimental $t_0$ annotations), the
    GOA\,Nonexp baseline can still contribute IEA, ISS, and other non-experimental
    terms. The KNN baseline has no self-exclusion disadvantage in this category
    (NK proteins with zero $t_0$ experimental annotations may still appear in
    the reference index, but they contribute only their non-experimental prior),
    so the gap is typically smaller.
  \item For \textbf{LK and PK proteins} (some or many $t_0$ experimental
    annotations), the GOA\,Nonexp baseline benefits from a richer non-experimental
    prior, widening the gap.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Temporal-Leakage Hygiene}

A critical constraint on the self-prior is the distinction between the
non-experimental $t_0$ annotations and the experimental $t_1$ annotations that
constitute the ground truth. Including experimental $t_0$ annotations in the
prior would constitute a form of label leakage: for NK proteins, there are no
experimental $t_0$ annotations by definition, so the constraint is automatically
satisfied. For LK and PK proteins, experimental $t_0$ annotations are excluded
from the prior because they would allow the system to directly recall known
experimental facts that should be treated as prior knowledge rather than
predictions.

The self-prior implementation (\cref{subsec:impl-goa-self-prior}) enforces
this by maintaining a hard exclusion list of experimental evidence codes
(\texttt{EXP}, \texttt{IDA}, \texttt{IMP}, \texttt{IGI}, \texttt{IEP},
\texttt{IPI}). Any candidate term derived from an excluded evidence code
is discarded before the candidate set is assembled, regardless of whether
the term would also be contributed by the KNN path. A subsequent check
verifies that the injected prior annotations carry a $t_0$ GOA release
identifier strictly earlier than the evaluation window boundary; annotations
that appeared at $t_1$ or later are never eligible.

\subsubsection{Effect on the Live-Board Gap}

On the live board, adding the leakage-safe GOA self-prior to the KNN submission
\emph{appeared} to narrow the gap to the GOA\,Nonexp baseline in the NK and LK
categories. This suggested a hypothesis: that a strong KNN retrieval method and a
leakage-safe self-prior might be complementary rather than competing, with the
prior capturing direct term-propagation evidence that retrieval misses by design
while the KNN step captures functional similarity to homologous proteins that the
prior cannot express. The live board is an unsealed surface, however, so the
observation was treated as a hypothesis rather than a result and tested under the
sealed protocol of this chapter. That test (\cref{subsec:exp12-lafa}) does
\emph{not} confirm it: fit on the SELECT window and sealed on the official target
frame, no score-level fusion of the self-prior generalises, and the apparent
board gain is best read as sampling noise. The sealed verdict supersedes the
live-board reading.

The combined method (KNN + leakage-safe self-prior) is implemented as a drop-in
post-processing step in the LAFA container entrypoint, requiring no change to the
PROTEA platform; the submission infrastructure is described in
\cref{sec:lafa-containers} and the extended container variant is designated
\texttt{protea-knn-v1+prior}. It is retained as a documented configuration rather
than the recommended scorer, in light of the sealed result above.

\paragraph{A frame-alignment audit.}

difference of annotation source, not of protocol: the benchmark derives its
experimental ground truth from \texttt{goa\_uniprot\_all} filtered to the
SwissProt-reviewed proteome, and PROTEA's local import of the same archive shows
almost no experimental growth across the window (only three of the 3{,}631 novel
PK terms the benchmark attributes to the missed proteins appear as experimental
in the local store). Reconstructing the target set locally therefore deflates PK
by leaving 1{,}322 targets unpredicted. The fix is to predict and score on the
benchmark's exact target list; all 7{,}401 targets are present in PROTEA's protein
store and carry embeddings, so no re-embedding is required.

neighbours subsume the protein's own electronic annotations, so the self-prior
contributes no separable signal at the score level. The apparent live-board gain
did not replicate on the SELECT window and is therefore read as sampling noise
rather than a real improvement. This is a negative result, recorded because it
delimits where the homology transfer is already saturated: extracting value from
the self-prior would require a learned re-ranker that models source agreement and
cross-aspect association rather than a score-level blend
(\cref{sec:conclusion-future-work}).

of truth for the reported figure.

% ── Experiment 13 ─────────────────────────────────────────────
\subsection{Experiment~13: A Learned Re-Ranker Reaches First Place}
\label{subsec:exp13-lafa-number-one}

Experiment~12 left the homology scorer level with the strongest method that uses
no learning, and traced the remaining distance to the leaders entirely to the two
categories where a protein is already partly characterised. It also named, as the
way to close that distance, a re-ranker that learns when to trust each source and
that exploits the functions a protein is already known to perform. This experiment
builds that re-ranker. Measured once on the same sealed target frame, and scored
with the benchmark's own harness, it places \emph{first} overall.

\paragraph{What the re-ranker does, in plain terms.}
The nearest-neighbour homology transfer of the previous chapters stays as the
backbone: it remains the part that does well when nothing else is known about a
protein. Three ideas are added on top, and a final learned stage decides how much
to trust each of them for each prediction.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{A second, complementary predictor.} Alongside the neighbour
    search, a model reads each protein's learned representation directly and
    proposes functions across the whole catalogue at once. Because it is not
    limited to the functions the neighbours happened to carry, it lifts the
    ceiling on what the system can recover.
  \item \textbf{Averaging away luck.} That predictor is trained several times from
    different starting points and its proposals are pooled. A single unlucky run
    can then no longer drive a prediction, which steadies the rarer and harder
    calls in particular. This pooling is, by construction, a Deep Ensemble; the
    ensembling rationale and its trade-offs are discussed in
    \cref{subsec:seed-ensemble}.
  \item \textbf{Using what a protein is already known to do.} For a protein that
    is already partly characterised, the system asks a simple question: which
    functions habitually accompany the ones this protein is already known to
    perform? It pays particular attention to associations that cross from one
    branch of the ontology to another, since those carry information that the
    protein's own sequence does not. This is precisely the cross-aspect signal
    that Experiment~12 identified as the missing ingredient, and it is the lever
    that carries the system past the previous leaders.
\end{enumerate}

The final stage is a learned combiner, fitted separately for each of the three
evaluation settings, that weighs the homology score, the direct predictor, the
protein's own weak prior annotations, and the association signal, together with
how informative and how common each candidate function is. Crucially, the
association signal is, by construction, silent for proteins about which nothing is
known: it can only speak when there is prior knowledge to speak from, so it cannot
leak any answer into the no-knowledge setting. A direct audit confirms this, the
known-term partition the signal reads from coincides exactly with the benchmark's
own definition of which proteins count as partly known.

\paragraph{Two figures: the research result and the productized result.}
This experiment reports two distinct numbers, and the thesis keeps them apart.
The first is the \emph{offline research result}: the sealed champion of the
project's offline laboratory, which reaches a mean IA-weighted micro-$F$ of
\textbf{0.391} on the official frame, ahead of the previously leading learned
systems. This is the method's sealed best and a first-class result, but it
carries the optimism that any single-window measurement does and its exact
training artefacts are no longer byte-for-byte reproducible, so it is reported
as a research result rather than as a figure the platform serves. The second is
the \emph{native productized result}: the same method reproduced end to end on
the PROTEA platform and scored through the live \texttt{/benchmark} surface,
which reaches a mean of \textbf{0.3745} (NK~0.4645, LK~0.4526, PK~0.2065). This
is the figure the application actually serves and that any reader can verify on
the running system. The offline research result is the headline; the native
productized result is the one the platform stands behind.

\paragraph{Result.}
\Cref{tab:exp13-lafa} reports the outcome on the official 7{,}401-target frame.
The offline research result places first overall, ahead of the previously

\paragraph{Where the remaining headroom actually lives.}
It is tempting to read the two unwon Biological Process cells as a limit of the
available evidence, and an earlier account of this work did exactly that. The
measurement does not support it. Three figures, obtained on a retrained booster
over an exported partly-known dataset and therefore reported as internal deltas
rather than as benchmark absolutes, locate the loss precisely. First, the evidence
is present: of the true partly-known protein to Biological Process term pairs the
system fails to predict, 97.0\% use a term that some protein in the same cohort
already carried before the target window opens, and 95.2\% once each term is
weighted by the information accretion the metric scores by, so only 3\% of the
pairs and 4.8\% of the weight are genuinely novel. Second,
retrieval is not what binds, even though the candidate pool recovers only 0.322 of
the true pairs. Submitting every pool cell that belongs to the propagated ground
truth, which asks what a perfect ordering of the candidates already retrieved would
be worth, yields $f_{\mu,w}=0.7519$ at precision 1.000, and the best achievable
ordering of that same pool reaches 0.7764. The reranker delivers 0.2131: the system
captures 27.4\% of what its own shortlist allows. Third, and consequently, the candidate-side
levers are inert. Expanding the pool by term co-occurrence lifts recall by half,
from 0.322 to 0.480, and converts to $+0.002$, because additional candidates do
not help a ranker that cannot order the ones it already holds.

The headroom is therefore one of ranking: 0.2131 against a shortlist worth 0.7764
to its best ordering, a factor of 3.6. Part of the mismatch is structural. The
reranker is trained with a listwise objective that optimises the order of
candidates within a single protein, whereas the benchmark's metric sweeps one
global threshold across every protein at once. A listwise objective is invariant
to any per-protein monotone rescaling of its scores, so it can order each protein
well and still leave those scores mutually incomparable across proteins.

The remedy that diagnosis suggests does not work. Retraining the identical rows
and features with a pointwise, globally calibrated objective scores $0.1518$ where
the deployed recipe scores $0.2121$ on the same rows: it costs $-0.06$ rather than
recovering anything. The reasoning is sound and the treatment it recommends is
measurably harmful, which is the first of several places in this section where a
convincing mechanism and a working lever part company.

What does pay is not calibration but abstention, and it follows from how the
metric reads a candidate that was never submitted. The evaluator propagates each
submission through the ontology and fills any term it did not receive with the
score of that term's best descendant, so a candidate withheld from the submission
does not score zero: its cell inherits, and those inherited ancestors arrive at a
weighted precision of 0.2967 against the arm's own 0.1801. A candidate submitted
with a low score is stored instead, and the fill leaves it alone, which forfeits
the inheritance. Submitting a weak candidate is therefore worse than submitting
nothing, and the deployed recipe had been exploiting this by accident: its scores
are unbounded margins, four in every five are at or below zero, and the evaluator
discards those, so the pipeline was abstaining on most of its own shortlist
without anyone choosing to.

Choosing it deliberately is worth a little more. Withholding the reranker's
weakest candidates at a threshold selected on nine of ten disjoint protein folds
and applied blind to the tenth gains $+0.0092$, with a fold-to-fold standard
deviation of $0.0034$ and a positive result in all ten folds. Scoring the
candidates proposed by the sparse classifier with that classifier's own output,
rather than with a reranker that never sees the features describing them, gains
$+0.0084$ (standard deviation $0.0058$, positive in nine of ten folds). The two
compose to $+0.016$. They are real, they cost nothing, and they do not close a
factor of 3.6.

The signal that would close it is not yet identified. The two structural
hypotheses testable with the resources of this work are both eliminated by
measurement: term co-occurrence as a candidate generator ($+0.002$) and Gene
Ontology hierarchical proximity as a feature (area under the curve 0.550,
decorrelated from the reranker yet contributing $+0.000$ when blended). A
text-aligned representation adds $+0.002$ on this cell and a domain-signature
graft is actively harmful on Biological Process, while a larger base
representation reorders the same candidates and so cannot help where recall is
not the constraint. The honest statement, and the one this work makes, is that
the frontier is characterised rather than crossed: its location is known, its
magnitude is quantified at a 3.6-fold ranking headroom inside the existing
shortlist, and the levers currently available are demonstrably insufficient to
reach it.

A methodological note deserves preservation, because it generalises beyond this
cell. The first reading of the 0.322 recall was that the system was recall
limited, and that reading was recorded before the oracle test was run. A recall
number alone does not identify what binds a pipeline; the ceiling of the pool
does, and obtaining it costs one evaluation with the labels used as the score.

\paragraph{Choosing on the past, measuring once on the future.}
Every choice that defines the system, which signals to include and how many
repeats to pool, was made on the earlier validation window (GOA~220~$\to$~227) and
the system was then measured a single time on the later target frame
(GOA~227~$\to$~230). To guard against the subtler risk of having chosen those
ingredients by watching the sealed score itself, the validation window was further
split into a fitting half and a held-out half that the final measurement never
touches. Re-deciding the ingredients on that internal split reproduces the same
verdict: each signal helps, the cross-aspect association helps the partly-known
settings most, and pooling more repeats helps. The improvements on the internal
split are real but smaller than on the target frame, so the first-place standing
is genuine while the precise size of the lead carries the single-window
uncertainty that any one measurement does. The honest reading is therefore a clear
first-place result driven by signals that are independently confirmed to help,
rather than a headline margin reported to the last digit.

\paragraph{Reproducibility.}
The full recipe, the trained combiners, and the exact-harness scorer are preserved
as a self-contained package in the project's offline laboratory. That offline
package is what produced the sealed 0.391 research figure; because its original
training artefacts have since been removed, that exact figure is a sealed research
result rather than a byte-for-byte reproducible one. The figure the platform
serves is the native productized reproduction (0.3745), which runs end to end on
the PROTEA platform and is exposed through the \texttt{/benchmark} surface
alongside the homology-only figure of Experiment~12, marked with the LAFA frame
and the sealed ($227 \to 230$) window. For that productized figure the
application is, as elsewhere in this chapter, the single source of truth: it is
the number a reader can confirm live on the running system.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Addressing the Research Questions}

\textbf{RQ1}: \emph{Can embedding-based KNN annotation transfer match or
exceed alignment-based methods for GO term prediction, particularly at low
sequence identity?}

Embedding-based KNN annotation transfer is competitive without any alignment
computation. Under the leakage-clean \texttt{cafaeval} protocol the 56-feature
re-ranker (Experiment~8) reaches an average $F_{\max}$ of \textbf{0.5427} across
all nine category-aspect cells, and the binary-label champion (Experiment~9)
pushes the NK+LK combined cafaeval $F_{\max}$ to \textbf{0.7291 $\pm$ 0.0028}
(three-seed replication, LB.3 paired bootstrap), the primary publishable result
of this thesis for the NK and LK categories. The directional evidence that
motivated this design is recorded in the development trace
(\cref{app:dev-trace}): an embedding-only baseline that already retrieves
function competitively at $k{=}5$, a heuristic alignment-weighted scoring step
that adds a small consistent gain over that baseline (Exp.~3), and a positioning
run against eggNOG-mapper in which the embedding retrieval leads in most NK and
LK cells (Exp.~7). Those early figures predate the leakage fix and the cafaeval
protocol, so they are kept in the appendix for provenance only and carry no live
\texttt{/benchmark} or \texttt{/evaluation} source; a controlled comparison of
the baseline, the heuristic, and eggNOG-mapper on a shared leakage-clean
SELECT-window split is future work, covered by the score-ablation track. The
multi-PLM $\times$ $K$ grid (FARM-EXP.13) provides the empirical evidence base
for assessing whether the champion result holds across alternative PLM
representations.

\textbf{RQ2}: \emph{Do pairwise alignment statistics and taxonomic distance
provide complementary signals that improve the ranking of candidate annotations?}

Partly, and the honest answer is more nuanced than a single dominant family.
The development trace (\cref{app:dev-trace}) showed, before the leakage fix,
that layering pairwise alignment statistics on top of the embedding-only
baseline yields a small consistent gain (Exp.~3), directional evidence that
alignment carries signal partially orthogonal to the learned representation.
The leakage-free full-feature
importance analysis (\cref{tab:feature-importance}) then locates the dominant
ranking signal elsewhere: vote-aggregation features
(\texttt{neighbor\_vote\_fraction}, \texttt{go\_term\_frequency}) lead in every
category, GO-embedding similarity (\texttt{anc2vec\_neighbor\_cos},
\texttt{anc2vec\_query\_known\_maxcos}) contributes prominently as a
complementary ontology-aware term, and raw protein-embedding distance falls
outside the top-10, used by the model as a candidate-generation filter rather
than a final ranking signal. Classical alignment and taxonomy features rank
low individually once embeddings and neighbourhood statistics are present, so
their marginal contribution is real but modest. The answer to RQ2 is therefore
qualified: alignment and taxonomy add a small, genuine complementary signal,
while the decisive ranking information comes from the statistical structure of
the retrieved neighbourhood.

A methodological caveat governs how this question must be read, and it is
itself one of the rigor findings of the work. An earlier 56-feature
leave-one-family-out ablation (Exp.~8, \cref{tab:exp8-ablation}) appeared to
show the \texttt{anc2vec\_query} family dominating by a wide margin. That
magnitude does not reflect biological signal. It was traced to a
replication-by-category construction artefact in the CAFA-style training-pair
export: each (query, candidate) pair was replicated once per category bucket
(NK, LK, PK) and relabelled per bucket, so \texttt{anc2vec\_query\_known\_count}
effectively encoded the provenance of a row (a genuine positive versus a
synthetic negative injected from another bucket) rather than any ontology-derived
property. The model learned this provenance shortcut, and the inflated ablation
delta is its footprint, not evidence of feature value. The artefact was
diagnosed and patched in PROTEA (commit \texttt{223299c}); datasets built after
the fix, including the \texttt{bench-v1-K5-filtered} set behind
\cref{tab:feature-importance}, do not exhibit it. No primary claim in this
chapter rests on the pre-fix ablation magnitude. A clean re-derivation of which
features carry genuine signal, scored on full ground truth with
information-accretion weighting and free of the replication artefact, is the
object of the score-ablation study reported in the experimentation track as
future work.

\textbf{RQ3}: \emph{How can a reproducible, scalable annotation pipeline be
designed so that researchers can re-run analyses as ontologies and databases are
updated?}

PROTEA's versioned data model ensures reproducibility by design. Every
prediction is linked to an immutable \texttt{EmbeddingConfig} UUID, a specific
\texttt{AnnotationSet} (versioned by GOA release number), and a specific
\texttt{OntologySnapshot} (versioned by OBO release date). The temporal holdout
training pipeline is itself parameterised by version numbers: re-running the
same \texttt{train\_reranker\_auto} payload against the same database state
produces identical models and evaluation results. When new GOA releases become
available, the pipeline can be extended by appending the new version to the
training list and shifting the test window forward.

\subsection{The Value of Feature Engineering in the Age of Embeddings}

A recurring theme in this evaluation is the interplay between protein language
model embeddings and classical sequence analysis features. The embedding-only
baseline captures broad functional similarity through the learned representation
space, but its ranking is limited to a single signal: cosine distance.

The LightGBM re-ranker, when given access to the full 56-feature set, learns
to combine multiple complementary signals:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Embedding distance} provides the initial KNN retrieval signal.
  \item \textbf{Alignment identity and scores} capture sequence-level conservation
    that the protein language model embedding may compress away.
  \item \textbf{Aggregation features} (vote fraction, GO term frequency) encode
    the statistical confidence of each candidate annotation.
  \item \textbf{GO-embedding similarity} (\texttt{anc2vec}) provides an
    ontology-aware signal complementary to protein-sequence embeddings.
  \item \textbf{Taxonomic distance} provides phylogenetic context.
  \item \textbf{Lineage features} (T-RES.1b; \texttt{lineage\_is\_ancestor\_of\_known},
    \texttt{lineage\_is\_descendant\_of\_known}, etc.) encode whether the candidate
    GO term is an ancestor or descendant of any term already known for the query
    protein.
\end{itemize}

The leakage-free full-feature importance analysis
(\cref{tab:feature-importance}) shows that vote-aggregation signals
(\texttt{neighbor\_vote\_fraction}, \texttt{go\_term\_frequency}) dominate
across all three categories, with GO-embedding similarity (\texttt{anc2vec})
contributing as a complementary ontology-aware term and raw protein-embedding
distance ranking outside the top-10. The much larger \texttt{anc2vec\_query}
ablation magnitude in the earlier 56-feature study
(\cref{tab:exp8-ablation}) is, as set out in the RQ2 answer above, a
replication-by-category dataset-construction artefact rather than evidence of
feature value; it is retained here as a methodological finding, patched in
PROTEA, and no conclusion in this section rests on it. With that caveat, the
leakage-clean evidence still supports the broader claim that feature
engineering remains valuable even when powerful embeddings are available: the
statistical structure of the retrieved neighbourhood and ontology-derived
similarity carry information not present in the raw protein-sequence embedding
alone. A clean per-feature re-derivation under full ground-truth
information-accretion scoring is provided by the score-ablation study reported
as future work.

\subsection{The Seed-Averaged Classifier as a Deep Ensemble}
\label{subsec:seed-ensemble}

The "averaging away luck" step of Experiment~13
(\cref{subsec:exp13-lafa-number-one}, item~2) deserves to be read against the
ensembling literature, because what it does has a precise name and a set of
known trade-offs. This subsection makes that connection explicit. It concerns
only the trained block of the pipeline, the M2 \texttt{anc2vec} classifier: a
hybrid multilayer perceptron that reads the 8320-dimensional concatenation of
the six protein language model embeddings and proposes GO terms across the whole
catalogue at once. The other two blocks ensemble under different physics and are
not the subject here. The composite KNN scorer is a similarity search, not a
trained model, so it has no optimisation noise to decorrelate by reseeding; its
diversity axes are instead $k$, the distance metric, the choice of PLM, the
pooling, and the aggregation rule, and in the leakage-clean validation of this
chapter those axes were explored and largely did not move the score. The
per-category LightGBM combiners do carry boosting randomness through row and
column subsampling, but their reported figures are seed-stable
(\cref{tab:exp9-champion-replication}), so the analysis below is specific to the
neural classifier.

What PROTEA does is train $K$ copies of that classifier from independent random
initialisations, with both a different initial weight draw and a different data
ordering per copy, and combine their \emph{output} probabilities: the candidate
set is the union of each member's top proposals, and a candidate's score is the
sum over the members that proposed it divided by the number of members. This is,
by construction, a Deep Ensemble in the sense of
Lakshminarayanan, Pritzel and Blundell~\cite{lakshminarayanan2017deepensembles}.
The class is attractive for two reasons that matter here: it is a strong
predictor, and its averaged probabilities are better calibrated than those of any
single member, which is the right property for a system whose downstream metric
thresholds confidence scores.

The choice to average in output space rather than weight space is deliberate, not
incidental. Independently initialised networks converge to different basins of
the loss landscape; their parameters are not coordinatewise comparable, and a
naive average of two such weight vectors generally lands in a high-loss region
between basins rather than at a sensible compromise. Weight-space averaging is
valid only when the points being averaged lie in a single basin, which is exactly
the regime that Stochastic Weight Averaging~\cite{izmailov2018swa} and Model
Soups~\cite{wortsman2022modelsoups} construct, the former by averaging iterates
along one trajectory, the latter by averaging fine-tunes that share an
initialisation. PROTEA's members share neither a trajectory nor an
initialisation, so output-space averaging is the only sound option for the
ensemble as built.

The size of the gain is governed by the bias-variance-ambiguity decomposition of
Krogh and Vedelsby~\cite{krogh1995ambiguity}: the ensemble's error is the average
member error minus an ambiguity term that grows with the diversity of the
members. Reseeding decorrelates only the optimisation noise, the part of each
member that depends on where it started and the order it saw the data, so it buys
a modest ambiguity, enough to steady the rarer and harder calls but not to change
the representation the members reason over. Larger ambiguity comes from
representation diversity. PROTEA's classifier already fuses the six PLMs by
\emph{early} fusion, concatenating them at the input, which is one diversity
mechanism baked into a single member; a \emph{late} fusion variant, a per-PLM
head whose outputs are averaged, would let the members disagree more and is the
natural way to raise ambiguity further. That direction is noted in the future
work (\cref{sec:conclusion-future-work}) rather than claimed here.

This framing also settles a reproducibility point that recurs through the thesis.
PROTEA does not search for and report a lucky seed; it fixes a converged set of
seeds and reports the average over them. The reported figure is therefore a
converged estimator of the ensemble mean, reproducible from zero by a third party
using \emph{any} seeds, and it carries a seed-variance interval rather than a
point that depends on a private random draw. This is the same principle that
governs the confidence-interval reporting elsewhere in this chapter: the quantity
of interest is an estimator with a stated uncertainty, not a single fortunate
run. The specific width of that interval is an empirical property of the trained
ensemble and is not asserted here.

The efficiency cost of a Deep Ensemble is honest to state: inference runs every
member, so the cost scales linearly in $K$, and the deployment carries $K$ sets
of weights rather than one. PROTEA accepted that cost because the objective was
predictive quality and calibration on a benchmark, not inference latency. Several
methods trade some of the predictive benefit for a single cheap forward pass, and
they are the natural design alternatives for a latency-bound deployment rather
than anything PROTEA did. Stochastic Weight Averaging~\cite{izmailov2018swa} and
an exponential moving average of the weights both collapse a trajectory of
iterates into one parameter set; Snapshot Ensembles~\cite{huang2017snapshot}
harvest several members from a single training run under a cyclic learning rate;
Model Soups~\cite{wortsman2022modelsoups} average a family of fine-tunes into one
model; and Monte Carlo Dropout~\cite{gal2016dropout} approximates an ensemble at
test time by sampling dropout masks. Of these, weight averaging (SWA or EMA) is
the most direct successor for PROTEA, since it would reduce the deployment to one
forward pass and one artifact, with the standing caveat that it is sound only
within a single basin and so would require the members to share a training
trajectory rather than being independently initialised as they are today.

\subsection{Limitations}
\label{subsec:limitations}

\begin{itemize}
  \item The temporal holdout strategy reduces contamination but does not
    eliminate it entirely: some test proteins share high sequence identity with
    reference proteins, making their annotations trivially predictable.
  \item ESMC~300M was chosen for computational tractability; larger variants
    would likely improve embedding quality at higher GPU cost.
  \item The evaluation considers only annotation transfer from Swiss-Prot
    experimental annotations; \gls{iea} annotations are excluded because they are
    themselves computationally derived.
  \item The IA file used (\texttt{IA\_cafa6.tsv}) was computed for the CAFA6
    challenge and may not perfectly reflect the information content of the
    specific GOA snapshots used here.
  \item The comparison with external tools is limited to eggNOG-mapper;
    additional tools (BLAST, InterProScan) would provide a more complete
    picture of the competitive landscape.
  \item The LK-BPO cell consistently favours the heuristic over the re-ranker,
    suggesting that the learned model may underperform for specific
    category-aspect combinations with limited positive examples.
  \item The Exp.~8 hyperparameter grid reveals a 0.0283 spread on NK-BPO
    (0.4386 to 0.4669), exceeding the informal robustness threshold of 0.02,
    indicating that optimal hyperparameters are cell-dependent and a joint
    per-cell tuning pass is warranted.
  \item The multi-PLM $\times$ $K$ generalisation grid (Exp.~9,
    \cref{tab:exp9-multiplm-grid}) is to be reported from the leakage-clean
    SELECT-window benchmark and is packaged via the F-DATA-PACK loop. The
    champion result (0.7291 $\pm$ 0.0028) is established on ProstT5 and does
    not depend on the grid; the grid provides breadth context for the
    generalisability claim.
\end{itemize}
