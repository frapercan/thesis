\subsection{Experiment~14: Where the Sparse Code Is Formed}
\label{sec:exp14-residue-sparse}

The encoder deployed in this system projects a protein's pooled embedding into a
sparse, ontology-aligned code, and retrieval then runs on that code rather than on
the dense vector. This experiment asks a question the deployed design never puts:
whether the sparse selection should happen \emph{before} the residues are pooled
rather than after.

The question is not cosmetic, because selection and averaging do not commute. Taking
the largest entries of an average is a different operation from averaging the largest
entries of each term, and the two disagree whenever an atom is a narrow runner-up in
many residues and a decisive winner in few. Pooling first lets such an atom bank
every second place it never won; selecting first never admits a second place at all.
Once a pooled vector exists the distinction is unrecoverable, since which residue
activated which atom has already been averaged away, so the two orders cannot be
compared by any post-hoc transformation of the stored embeddings. They can only be
compared by running the language model again.

\subsubsection{Design}

All encoders are fitted on the same proteins, with the same objective, budget and
random seed, and scored on the same held-out proteins that no encoder has seen. The
population being scored is the temporal evaluation delta; the proteins that the
deployed encoder was itself fitted on are removed before anything is measured, since
scoring it on those would credit it with an advantage that has nothing to do with the
mechanism under test.

Two references are reported for every arm, because there are two questions with
different answers. Against a \emph{pooled control}, an encoder fitted on the same
proteins with the same objective and budget, the comparison isolates the mechanism:
whatever an arm wins there is not explained by having seen more data or a different
loss. Against the \emph{deployed encoder}, frozen, the comparison asks whether any of
it improves on what the system currently runs, a coarser question in which the
training budget is part of the comparison rather than a confound to be removed.

The primary screen is the mean functional similarity between a protein and its ten
nearest neighbours by code cosine, over ancestor closures propagated through the
ontology. It is used because it is what nearest-neighbour transfer consumes, and its
validity as a proxy is tested rather than assumed in
Section~\ref{sec:exp14-fmax}. Every difference carries a bootstrap interval paired on
proteins, and every result is reported by protein length band.

\subsubsection{A resolution floor, measured rather than assumed}
\label{sec:exp14-floor}

Before any arm is ranked, the study's own reproducibility is measured. The same
recipe, the same seed, fitted twice on two extractions verified identical to the
last bit, produces encoders that differ by $0.0013$ in mean purity, with a paired
interval that excludes zero.

That number is not noise in the estimator; it is variance in the fit. A bootstrap
paired on proteins resamples the population and is silent about the optimiser, and
two mechanisms feed the gap: the order in which minibatches arrive, and the fact that
a selection of the largest entries is unstable under it. A shift of one part in ten
million swaps a near-tied atom, and the code then differs by that atom's whole
magnitude. An independent estimate agrees: two aggregation routes that are
algebraically identical for proteins occupying a single window score $0.0006$ apart,
again with a separating interval.

Every difference below roughly $0.0015$ is therefore reported as unresolved
regardless of its interval. Three of the intervals in this section exclude zero and
are nonetheless declared inconclusive on that basis. Stating the floor first, rather
than discovering it when a favourable difference needs defending, is what makes the
ranking that follows meaningful.

\subsubsection{Interventions that did not help}

Table~\ref{tab:exp14-negatives} collects the changes that were expected to help and
did not. They are reported because each one closes a direction, and because two of
them contradict standard practice.

\begin{table}[htbp]

Hard negative mining, which the deployed encoder uses, costs accuracy here. The
mechanism is specific rather than mysterious: the objective is a regression that asks
the code cosine to equal a functional similarity, and the calibration of that scale
is carried by the mass of easy pairs. Oversampling the pairs the code currently gets
wrong biases the estimate toward suppressing cosines and distorts the scale it is
trying to fit. A ranking loss, tried as the natural alternative, was worse still, and
because it was worse for signed codes as well as for binary ones the failure is
attributable to the objective and not to an interaction with the code. The lesson is
that the objective and the sampling scheme are one decision and cannot be chosen
apart.

Binary residue values fail for a reason that is geometric and was confirmed by direct
measurement rather than inferred from the score. A code whose entries cannot be
negative has cosines confined to a narrow, high band, while the functional
similarities it is asked to reproduce are concentrated near zero. It is not that the
representation is imprecise; it is that it cannot express dissimilarity at all. The
practical consequence is that a quantised code must keep its sign, and that a
representation of this kind cannot be trained by ordinary backpropagation at all,
since replacing a value by a membership indicator leaves the output independent of
the weights and the backward pass finds no path.

Aggregating at the window rather than the residue also loses, and it loses most
clearly where it can act: on the proteins long enough to occupy more than one window
it is five times worse than its diluted figure over the whole population. Giving each
window equal weight discards the fact that windows hold different numbers of
residues, which per-residue pooling already respected.

Finally, the size of the training corpus appeared to saturate early, and the
appearance was a property of the control rather than of the corpus. Fifteen thousand
proteins to sixty thousand moved less than the resolution floor, which invited the
conclusion that the size lever was exhausted. Two choices in that measurement cut the
same way. The arms were scored on a population they had not been trained on, and this
study measures separately that a population mismatch costs an encoder its entire
advantage. The training budget was held at a fixed number of steps, which is the
natural control for separating data diversity from compute, and which also grants the
small arm five hundred and seventy six passes over its data and the large arm thirty
eight.

Repeating the comparison as a factorial over both choices settles it, and settles it
cheaply, because the fits were already on disk and every cell is therefore a scoring
pass rather than a training run. Table~\ref{tab:exp14-size-factorial} gives the same
step, measured four times.

Neither factor accounts for the result on its own. Each moves the step by about three
thousandths, and together they move it by eighteen. The mechanism is plain once it is
stated: additional data pays only if the budget lets the model absorb it, and what was
absorbed shows only where it applies. Starve either and the curve looks flat. A fixed
step count is not a neutral control when the arms differ in how much data they hold,
and the conclusion drawn from it was drawn in the one cell of four where the effect
cannot be seen.

What the corrected curve does not do is overturn the decision it was asked to inform.
On the population the system actually serves, four times the corpus buys under four
thousandths of neighbour quality, and the smallest difference this study can convert
into a detectable change in maximum F-measure is six times larger than that. The
corpus is worth extending, and it is not worth extending for this. That conclusion is
now measured rather than assumed, which is the only respect in which it improves on
the one it replaces.

A second axis was never varied at all. The training signal is a similarity between
annotation sets, so a protein carrying a combination of terms already present adds
sequence diversity at unchanged supervision, while one carrying a new combination adds
supervision as well. Counting them apart separates the two. The vocabulary grows
sublinearly, with an exponent near one third, and the number of terms per protein is
flat at forty six across every corpus size, so quadrupling the corpus adds about forty
five per cent more vocabulary and no density at all. Yet of the proteins the step adds,
seven in eight carry no term that was absent before, yet more than a third carry a
combination of terms never seen. What a larger corpus supplies is not new functions. It
is new arrangements of known ones, which is the protein-by-term incidence relation and
not either of the two sets that compose it.

\subsubsection{Does capacity pay once there is enough data to feed it?}

An earlier section of this experiment established that capacity is not the constraint:
a non-linear map, a wider dictionary and a second learned stage each fit the training
set better and none of them generalised. Every one of those arms was fitted on under
four thousand proteins. Since the corpus axis turned out to be alive after all, the
cross that neither measurement made is the one that matters, and a non-linear map at
sixty thousand proteins answers it.

Capacity pays, and it pays only where the training data lies. On the corpus-like
population the non-linear map is worth four and a half times the resolution floor, a
gain the same architecture could not produce at four thousand proteins because there
was not enough data for the extra parameters to have anything to fit. On the population
the system serves the same two maps are indistinguishable. The result is not that the
earlier finding was wrong, but that it was stated more generally than it had been
measured: capacity is not the constraint for anything visible at serving time, and that
is the claim the recipe rests on. The deployable map therefore stays linear, at half the
parameters, because it ties where ties are decided.

The mechanism is worth recording because it is not the usual one. The non-linear arm
does not fit the training objective better: both arms finish within a ten thousandth of
each other on training loss. Whatever the extra capacity buys, it is not memorisation of
the training set, and by length the whole of the in-domain gain sits in the proteins
shorter than five hundred residues while the longer bands fall below the floor.

\subsubsection{What the whole corpus would be worth}

The three measured sizes support an extrapolation one decade beyond the largest of them,
which is what the full pool of five hundred and fifty seven thousand candidates would
represent. A logarithmic model, two parameters against three points, tracks them to
within four ten thousandths, under the resolution floor. It puts the full corpus at
between four and seven thousandths of neighbour quality above sixty thousand, which
would take the advantage over the deployed encoder from about fourteen thousandths to
around twenty.

The question that settles the matter is the inverse one. A map fitted on 3,964 proteins
drawn from the population being scored beats a map fitted on sixty thousand corpus
proteins by nearly thirteen thousandths on that population. Asking what corpus size
would close that gap, under the model that assumes the curve never saturates and is
therefore the generous one, returns three and three quarter million proteins: seven
times every candidate that exists. Asking what size would convert into a detectable
change in maximum F-measure returns a number two orders of magnitude larger than that.

So the corpus axis closes by arithmetic rather than by a measurement that a better
experiment could overturn, and it closes in the opposite direction from the one the
first measurement suggested. The corpus is not exhausted. It is simply the wrong thing
to buy, because the quantity it competes against, agreement between the training and
serving populations, is available at fifteen times less data and is worth six times more.
Pre-training on the large corpus and adapting in domain remains worth nothing, and this
now survives the corrected protocol: adapting from the converged sixty thousand map
rather than the starved one moves the result by thirteen ten thousandths, below the
floor.

\subsubsection{Does the training population convert into the task?}

Every result to this point is neighbour purity, and this chapter has already recorded one
occasion where a large gain on that screen produced none on maximum F-measure. The
training-population axis is the widest the study has: nearly three hundredths of purity
separate its smallest arm from its largest, so if the screen predicts the task anywhere
it predicts it here, and if it fails here the axis carries nothing.

The measurement is the task itself. A reference bank of the sixty thousand annotated
corpus proteins, each held-out query retrieving its nearest neighbours by code cosine,
their propagated terms transferred with a score that is the similarity mass supporting
each term over the total, scored by maximum F-measure. Three things separate it from the
chapter's earlier attempt. It is stratified by knowledge category, because the served
population is roughly one twentieth of an evaluation window and an aggregate over the
whole of one is nine tenths a prior-knowledge score. The prior-knowledge exclusion is
applied, so a protein that already had annotations is scored on what it gained. And it
retrieves ten neighbours rather than thirty, following the earlier finding in this
chapter.

Nothing separates. Every interval spans zero, molecular function is negative in almost
every arm, and, more tellingly than any single comparison, the arms do not order: the
three hundredths of purity that separate the smallest training set from the largest
produce no consistent difference between them on the task, with conversion slopes running
from a fifth negative to a half positive across aspects and neighbour counts.

The temptation is to report that as a result, and it is not one. The served cell holds
between fifty five and eighty eight proteins depending on aspect, the paired interval is
forty six thousandths wide, and the effect the purity gain predicts is one and one fifth
times its half width. At that ratio a real effect of the expected size would be seen
about half the time. \textbf{This is an absence of demonstration and not a demonstration of
absence}, and the distinction is not pedantry: it decides whether the next thing to buy
is a better encoder or a bigger evaluation.

It is the latter, and enlarging it turned out to cost less than the argument for doing
it. The split was stratified by length, which was right for its purpose and happened to
put half the no-knowledge proteins into training. Returning them to the evaluation, and
extracting the two hundred and fifty the probe never carried, takes the cell from one
hundred and forty proteins to six hundred and forty nine for thirty six seconds of card
time. It also makes the measurement cleaner than it was, since no arm is then fitted on
any protein of the category it is judged on, which is the situation at serving time: the
one thing a query never has is experimental evidence about itself.

\subsubsection{What the enlarged cell shows, and what it costs the argument}

Two findings, and the second is the more important one.

\textbf{The screen does predict the task, within an architecture.} Two arms differing
only in how many proteins they were fitted on order the same way on both, in all three
aspects, at slopes between a third and seven tenths. The null of the previous section was
a failure of resolution and not a property of the encoder, exactly as its own interval
warned it might be.

\textbf{And the screen inverts between architectures.} The smaller arm has more neighbour
purity than the deployed encoder and less maximum F-measure, and both of those
differences separate.

\begin{table}[htbp]
  \centering
  \caption{Neighbour purity and maximum F-measure against the deployed encoder, in the
  served no-knowledge cell of 649 proteins, at ten neighbours. Purity is measured on
  proteins of other categories, so no arm is scored on what it was fitted on. Intervals

  \label{tab:exp14-screen-vs-task}
  \input{chapters/tables_experiment14/fmax_big_cell}

Read the first row on its own and it is a contradiction: more of the quantity that is
supposed to predict the task, significantly less of the task. Read the table as a whole
and it is not a contradiction but a boundary. Neighbour purity counts how many retrieved
neighbours share terms with the query. The transfer does not count neighbours, it weights
them by similarity, and two architectures that retrieve equally good neighbour sets can
distribute similarity over them quite differently. Within one architecture that
distribution is roughly fixed and the count predicts the outcome. Across architectures it
is not, and the count stops meaning the same thing on both sides of the comparison.

The consequence for this chapter is direct and it is not comfortable. The headline it
opened with, that a residue-level sparse code beats the deployed pooled encoder by
between two and three hundredths, is a purity statement made across exactly the boundary
where purity stops being comparable. On the task, in the cell the system serves, the
recipe ties what is deployed: molecular function, biological process and cellular
component all fail to separate.

What survives is smaller and firmer than what it replaces. The representation reaches the
deployed encoder's task performance while occupying two hundred and fifty six bytes per
protein against four hundred and thirty two, or seventy seven at one bit with a calibrated
margin. Parity at a fraction of the storage is a result worth having, and it is one a
storage-blind screen could never have established either way. The methodological lesson
generalises past this chapter: a screen chosen because it is what the downstream consumer
reads is safe for ranking variants of one design and unsafe the moment the design itself
is the variable.

\subsubsection{Do the layers of the language model carry different signal?}

A comparison across layers is impossible on raw activations, and the reason is worth
recording. The middle layers of the backbone used here carry activations four orders
of magnitude larger than the last: typical vector norms run from under two at the
output to nearly one hundred thousand in the middle. A map initialised and optimised
identically across such a range is not in the same regime for each layer, so a first
comparison ranked the input scale and not the layer, and it silently broke the
dictionary comparison as well, since a concatenation of four layers at those relative
scales simply is the two largest of them.

After standardising each layer with statistics fitted on the training split alone,
the ordering is monotone in depth and the last layer wins. A learned mixture over
layers, initialised at an equal average and free to move, converges to weights that
increase monotonically with depth and places nearly half its mass on the final layer;
no combination of layers beats that layer alone, and the best of them merely ties it
within the floor. The practical consequence is that extracting further layers is not
worth its cost, and that a single layer is both the best and the cheapest input.

This disagrees with an earlier measurement in this work, which placed the middle
layers ahead. That measurement was made on pooled embeddings and a different task,
and the two are not the same question. The disagreement is recorded rather than
resolved, because resolving it would require rerunning the earlier protocol under the
present one and no such run was made.

\subsubsection{The cell the system is for}
\label{sec:exp14-category}

The figures above are aggregates over an evaluation set, and an evaluation set is not
the population this system serves. Everything scored so far carries annotation
already, because scoring requires it. The system exists for proteins that carry none.

The measurable stand-in is the no-knowledge cell, and it is small. In the window used
here it holds 588 protein-by-aspect cells against 942 with limited knowledge and 7,059
with prior knowledge; inside the held-out subset, 208 against 343 and 2,502. \textbf{So an
aggregate over this set is nineteen parts in twenty a prior-knowledge score}, and it
is silent about the cell the work is for. Reporting by category is not a refinement of
the result, it is the difference between measuring the task and measuring something
adjacent to it.

Two protocol details separate a real evaluation from a flattering one, and both were
wrong in the aggregate reported earlier. The truth must be what a protein GAINED over
the window and not the propagated closure of the reference release, because that
closure contains everything already known about a protein and a neighbour transfer
reproduces it trivially. And the prior-knowledge cell carries an exclusion list of
terms already known, 307,011 of them here, about forty-three per cell. Applying it
drops the prior-knowledge maximum F-measure from roughly 0.53 to 0.13, and that drop
is the size of the credit an unexcluded evaluation gives away.

It does not follow that the proposed encoder is worse. Every interval includes zero,

effects above roughly three hundredths would be visible. This is a null with weak

available windows: they are nested, the longest containing the shorter ones, so they
hold the same proteins under different truth and pooling them would count each twice.
Only further windows in time, which the campaign produces, add independent cells.

\subsubsection{How many bits a stored value needs}
\label{sec:exp14-bits}

A code that is better is only deployable if it is also affordable to store and to
scan. The retrieval format holds a code as pairs of an atom index and its value, so a
dictionary of this width costs eleven bits to name an atom and the value precision is
what remains negotiable.

Two ways of reducing it were compared. Quantising the code after training is free to
implement and loses whatever it loses. Training with the quantiser inside the forward
pass, with the gradient routed straight through it, is the standard remedy and is
expected to recover most of that loss.

It does the opposite. Training with the quantiser is worse at four bits, at two and
at one, and by an increasing margin as the levels get coarser. Two mechanisms are
plausible and neither was verified: the straight-through gradient ignores that the
scale is itself a function of the code being quantised, so the objective moves with
the map; and the estimator here must pass through two stacked discrete operations
rather than one, since a selection of the largest entries already sits below the
quantiser. The finding is reported as an empirical negative, because a plausible
mechanism for a result is not the same as having measured it.

Applied after training, the picture is favourable and aspect-dependent
(Table~\ref{tab:exp14-quantisation}). Four bits is indistinguishable from half
precision on all three aspects while removing forty-four per cent of the bytes, and
it still improves on the deployed encoder on all three with separating intervals. Two
bits removes half the bytes and costs nothing measurable on biological process, which
is the aspect this thesis cares most about, but it gives back the whole gain on
molecular function and lands in a tie with the deployed system there. Four bits is
therefore the operating point, and two bits is available when storage is the binding
constraint and that trade is acceptable.

One accounting caveat belongs with the byte figures. They describe the sparse format
that a retrieval scan reads, where a code is a list of indices and values. Stored as a
dense vector in the database column, the same code occupies its full width regardless
of how few atoms are non-zero and regardless of their precision, so this saving is one
on the served bank and on scan bandwidth, not on the table.

This also settles, for a second time and by a cleaner contrast, what went wrong with
the binary residue values of Table~\ref{tab:exp14-negatives}. A signed one-bit code
scores far above the non-negative binary one at the same precision. The obstruction
was never how few levels there are; it was that a code confined to non-negative
values cannot express dissimilarity. Two remedies proposed for that diagnosis, a
ranking objective and quantisation-aware training, were both measured and both failed.
A correct account of a mechanism does not license the first cure that follows from it.

\subsubsection{A population nobody looked at, and what it took back}
\label{sec:exp14-confirmation}

Roughly forty encoding arms were compared on one held-out set. Held out is not the
same as untouched: every choice in this section, the number of atoms, the layer, the
sampling, the precision, was made while looking at that set, and with forty
comparisons on a fixed sample a winner wins partly by chance. No interval repairs
that, because the bias is in the selection and not in the estimate.

The corpus contains half a million annotated proteins that no arm of this study and
no version of the deployed encoder has seen. Three thousand were drawn from them by a
fixed stride, a prediction was written down before the draw, and three encoders were
run once with no tuning of any kind.

\paragraph{The prediction failed, and then failed differently than expected.} The
proposed encoder had beaten the deployed one by three hundredths of purity on the
tuning population. On the confirmation population it scored below the resolution
floor. That collapse was neither a change in the mix of protein lengths, since it
happened within every length band, nor a ceiling, since the deployed encoder's own
margin over the dense vector was larger there and not smaller.

It was the training population. The same architecture fitted on corpus-like proteins,
for which the confirmation set is in domain, retrieves neighbours far better than the
deployed encoder there, by nine hundredths of purity with a wide separating interval,
about three times the largest advantage ever measured on the tuning set. An encoder
fitted on one population simply does not transfer to another, which this section had
already measured in a weaker form and which the author had failed to draw the
consequence from.

\paragraph{And the metric did not move at all.} That is the finding of this section,
and it is uncomfortable. Nine hundredths of neighbour purity produced no change in the
maximum F-measure. On the confirmation population the proposed encoder and the
deployed one are indistinguishable on all three aspects, while both remain clearly
above the dense vector (Table~\ref{tab:exp14-confirmation}).

The mechanism is visible in the relationship itself. From the dense vector to the
deployed encoder, two hundredths of purity buy nearly two hundredths of F-measure. From
the deployed encoder to the in-domain proposal, nine hundredths of purity buy nothing.
The confirmation population is easier in absolute terms, with F-measures above eight
tenths against five to seven on the delta, and once the reference bank holds close
homologs any competent encoder finds them. Beyond that point the ceiling is set by the
transfer and not by the retrieval, and better neighbours stop being worth anything.

\paragraph{What has to be withdrawn.} Neighbour purity is not a reliable proxy for the
maximum F-measure. It tracked the task on one population and saturated on another, and
the claim in Section~\ref{sec:exp14-fmax} that the ordering transfers holds for those
proteins only. Eleven axes of this study were refined on that screen, so each of the
recommendations that follows from them is a recommendation about retrieval quality and
is only a recommendation about annotation quality where the two were checked to agree.
The recipe was checked, on both populations, and its result is stated with the
population named.

\subsubsection{How far short, measured in the units that matter}
\label{sec:exp14-conversion}

A null invites two readings and they lead to opposite decisions. Either better
retrieval stops paying beyond some point, in which case this line of work is finished,
or the improvements obtained here are simply too small to register, in which case the
line is open and the useful question is how much larger they would have to be.

The two are separated by putting every method on one axis. The horizontal coordinate
is the quality of the neighbours a method actually returns, measured as the functional
overlap between those neighbours and what the query gained, under the same aspect
restriction and the same exclusion the metric uses. The vertical coordinate is the
maximum F-measure that method reaches. Adding the oracle of
Section~\ref{sec:exp14-category} gives the far end of the axis, and the shape between
the points answers the question.

The conversion is linear with a median slope of 0.94. One unit of neighbour quality is
worth about one unit of maximum F-measure across the whole range, so nothing saturates
and there is no point beyond which better neighbours stop paying. The first reading is
therefore wrong, and the second is right: the improvements are too small.

How small is now a number rather than an impression. Against the deployed encoder the
best arm here gains between seven and fifteen thousandths of neighbour quality, and
the smallest gain that would be visible in the no-knowledge cell is between
twenty-four and thirty-seven thousandths. That is a factor of between two and four,
and the oracle leaves more than a third of the axis unused in the cell that matters
most.

\paragraph{An inflation this exposes in the study's own screen.} The functional screen
used to refine the eleven axes above reports the same encoders as gaining nine
hundredths. It scores the mean overlap with the FULL propagated closure of the
reference release, pooled over aspects, and a protein's closure contains everything
already known about it. Measured the way the metric defines functional similarity,
that gain is roughly ten times smaller. The screen was not wrong about the ORDER of
the arms, which is what it was used for, but it was wrong about the SIZE by an order
of magnitude, and any target set from it would have been set an order of magnitude too
low. The lesson generalises past this study: a proxy has to be calibrated in the
target's units before a quantity measured on it is treated as a quantity.

\subsubsection{What this establishes, and what it does not}

Two claims survive both populations, and they are narrower than the tuning set alone
would have supported.

The mechanism is worth more than the data. At a matched corpus, matched size, and with
the deployed encoder additionally enjoying a mining scheme the proposed one does not
use, moving the selection to the residue level improves retrieval, and it does so by
roughly four times what multiplying the training corpus by fifteen is worth. That
ratio, rather than any single score, is the finding.

What that buys in annotation quality depends on the population, and the dependence is
not a caveat but a result. On the evaluation delta, the population that is actually
submitted, the maximum F-measure improves on all three aspects with separating
intervals. On the general corpus it does not move, while retrieval improves sharply.
The honest statement is therefore conditional, and any deployment of this encoder has
to name the population it serves and check the metric there rather than inheriting a
number from here.

Three limitations bound it. The evaluation population is one temporal window and one
reference bank, so the result is a measurement of this environment and not a general
claim about protein language models. The screen and the metric agree on one of the two
populations tested and disagree on the other, so every axis refined on the screen
alone is established for retrieval and open for annotation. And the comparison holds the backbone fixed throughout; nothing here says
whether a different backbone would move the same axes in the same directions, and the
one axis that was varied within the backbone, the choice of layer, was enough to show
that such questions can invert when a confound of scale is removed.
